The problem we solve, the job of the app, and where it goes next.

Freedom Planner is not another expense tracker and not a product catalogue. It exists so a household in India can see surplus, debt, cover, tax, and FIRE as one picture — then act on that picture. This page is the internal brief. The Arjun Mehta walkthrough is at /guide.

Read the problem Jump to the vision

Problem statement

Households already have the pieces of a financial life. They do not have one system that turns those pieces into answers they can use.

Exact problem: a salaried or self-employed adult in India cannot see, in one place, what is left after EMIs and SIPs, whether debt and insurance match their income and dependents, whether investments fund their goals and FIRE date, or which tax regime is cheaper for their year. Advice and product sales happen without that picture.

Freedom Planner · the split picture we are replacing
Where it lives today What they still cannot see
Salary in the bank app True monthly surplus after EMIs, premiums, and SIPs
EMIs in another app or Excel Debt burden, and which loan is the expensive one
SIPs on a broker or AMC site Whether the portfolio actually funds the dated goal
Policy PDFs in email Cover vs income, dependents, and outstanding loans
Tax in a CA spreadsheet or last year’s return Old vs new regime with this year’s deductions
Advice from relatives, YouTube, or a relationship manager A plan tied to their numbers, not a product quota

The problem is not “people do not have apps.” They have too many fragments and no household ledger.

What that causes

Surplus, emergency months, insurance adequacy, and retirement year stay guesses. “Start a SIP” or “buy term” is generic. A new job, EMI, or child does not update a plan because there is no plan — only scattered balances.

What we are not solving

We are not building live bank aggregation, PAN/Aadhaar KYC, tax filing, or a marketplace as the first job. Those can come later. The first job is a trusted picture of the household.

Objective of the app

Give that adult a living financial picture — then turn it into a forecast and a plan of action that is computed from their records, not guessed in a chat.

  1. 1
    Capture the household

    Profile, income, expenses, loans, investments, insurance, a required emergency fund, one FIRE path (Lean, Fat, or Coast), and other goals. Defaults: ₹, 6% inflation, retire at 60.

  2. 2
    Show one picture

    Net worth, surplus, debt-to-income, coverage, and goal progress on a dashboard. Bank or UPI statements become categorized lines. Tax compares old vs new for their year.

  3. 3
    Project and advise

    A deterministic cash-flow / net-worth engine, then a structured AI report on that snapshot, plus a PDF they can keep. Suggestions must name the expensive loan, the cover gap, or the unfinished emergency fund — not generic tips.

Who it is for

Salaried or self-employed adult in India who wants FIRE, a house, education funding, or simply not to be surprised by EMIs and tax.

What “done” means today

They finish setup, see a coherent dashboard from saved records, regenerate an advisor report when numbers change, and download a PDF.

How we stay honest

Planner math is the source of the picture. AI fills a schema on top of that math. We do not let a model invent surplus or EMI.

What the app does today

This is the working product. Use it as the contract for what “Freedom Planner” means before any distribution work.

Freedom Planner · surfaces in the app

Manage

Income, expenses, daily tracker, loans, investments, insurance, goals.

Plan

Statements, tax planner, freedom calculator, forecast, AI advisor, learning hub.

Users sign up with OTP, complete Quick Setup, then move Overview → Manage → Plan → Report. See the sample household on /guide.

Vision for later

Once the ledger is trusted, the same picture can recommend the next action — and later let the user take it in-app. The plan stays primary. A loan, policy, or fund is offered only when the household numbers support it.

Freedom Planner · future sequence, not a product banner

Need → product

Sell only what the plan already says they need.

Cover

Insurance

If term or health is short vs income and dependents, quote and buy (or renew) through a licensed partner, then write the policy back onto the insurance list. Do not push a policy when cover is already enough.

Goals

Mutual funds

If emergency months are done and surplus remains, start or raise a goal SIP. Skip equity when the buffer is empty or EMI ratio is already high. New SIPs must appear on the investment list so the planner stays true.

Debt

Loans

Refinance or balance-transfer when a cheaper EMI is justified. Top-up only when surplus and a dated goal support it. If unsecured EMI is already heavy, the correct product is “do not borrow.”

Insurance gap first, then goal SIPs, then loan refinance. That order matches how cash flow actually works. Partners transact; we keep the plan.

Why these three

Banks, insurers, and AMCs sell them as separate products. For a household they are one surplus decision. Only a ledger that already holds loans, cover, and investments can sequence them honestly.

How we earn later

Need-based distribution: origination on loans, commission on insurance, trail on funds — through licensed partners. Extra AI reports can stay a product fee. We do not become the lender or the insurer.

What stays out until we mean it

Native mobile, live bank/UPI aggregation, household sharing, in-app KYC, and tax filing are later platform work. They are not required to prove the core loop.
