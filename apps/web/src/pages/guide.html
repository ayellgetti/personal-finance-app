How to get started

After you create an account, Quick Setup asks for a financial profile. Income, expenses, loans, investments, and insurance are optional on each step. An emergency fund and one FIRE path (Lean, Fat, or Coast) are required before you can finish.

  1. 1
    Sign up with email and mobile OTP

    Your money is stored on the server for your account, not in a spreadsheet.

  2. 2
    Complete Quick Setup

    Profile → income → expenses → loans → investments → insurance → emergency fund → FIRE path → other goals.

  3. 3
    Read the dashboard

    Net worth, surplus, debt ratio, and a projected freedom date appear from the records you saved.

  4. 4
    Open AI Advisor and the report

    Suggestions are tied to your numbers: prepay high-cost debt, fill cover gaps, keep SIPs when cashflow allows.

The sample household: Arjun Mehta

Arjun is a 32-year-old salaried tech lead in India, with two dependents, a home loan, and a FIRE target at 55. The sample is fictional. It is sized so every module has something to show: cash flow, debt, SIPs, cover, and goals.

Profile

Age 32 · retire at 55 · salaried · 2 dependents · inflation 6% · currency ₹

What he wanted

A single view of surplus, whether EMIs are too high, if term cover is enough, and when FIRE is realistic.

What was messy

Salary, rent, SIPs, three loans, LIC, and school fees lived in memory. He could not see the trade-offs.

What looked healthy

41% savings rate (above the 20% 50-30-20 floor). DTI 27% (under 40%). Eight investment types. SIPs of ₹98,500 already running. Term cover exists, even if it is short.

What the gauges flagged

Emergency fund ~4.8 of 6 months. Term ₹1.50 Cr vs roughly 12× income + loans (~₹4.13 Cr) or the 15×-income coaching rule. Health cover ₹10 L vs ~₹22.5 L for three people. Equity share ~46% vs ~68% at age 32.

The problems the sample made obvious

Arjun felt “fine” because salary was high. The platform showed cashflow, cost of debt, and protection as one picture. That is the job of the sample: teach what to look at before you type your own data.

1. Surplus was already spoken for

₹1.04 L leftover after expenses, EMIs, and premiums, but ₹98,500 already goes to SIPs. Almost no cash left to fix the emergency gap or the expensive loan.

2. One loan was much costlier

Personal loan at 13.5% on ₹2.20 L. Home loan 8.5% is cheaper than equity’s planned 12% return, so blasting the home loan is not the first move.

3. Cover did not match income

Two dependents, ₹3.03 Cr annual income, ₹49 L debt. ₹1.50 Cr term and ₹10 L health leave a gap if income stops.

4. Emergency fund was incomplete

Essentials plus EMIs are ~₹1.32 L/month. Six months needs ~₹7.94 L. He has ₹6.30 L liquid + earmarked. Close, not done.

How the platform answered — a plan of action

Advisor output is JSON from your planner snapshot (rules plus optional OpenAI). For Arjun the rule engine already supports a balanced plan: do not pause all SIPs just because debt exists; attack the expensive loan; finish the safety buffer; close insurance gaps.

Freedom Planner · AI Financial Advisor · sample plan

Plan of action

Keep investing. Fix the 13.5% loan, the 6-month buffer, and term cover first.

High

Prepay the personal loan

₹2.20 L at 13.5%, 22 months left. Redirect a slice of surplus (and the ₹4,000 crypto SIP if needed) until this EMI of ₹11,200 disappears. Do not treat the 8.5% home loan the same way.

High

Lift term cover toward 15× income

Coaching rule: ₹4.55 Cr–₹6.06 Cr. Current term ₹1.50 Cr. Add cover sized to income plus outstanding loans, then keep paying the existing SIPs.

Medium

Finish the 6-month emergency fund

Shortfall about ₹1.64 L vs the salaried 6× essentials+EMI target. Sample contribution ₹25,000/month closes it in a few months. Park it in liquid instruments, not equity.

Medium

Raise family health cover

₹10 L floater vs ~₹22.5 L suggested for three people. Sample expiry is soon — renew and top up together. Car insurance is a date reminder, not a wealth lever.

Keep

Continue scheduled SIPs after the buffer

DTI is 27%, savings rate 41%. Home-loan ROI is 8.5%. The planner does not require pausing index/EPF SIPs. After the personal loan is gone, that ₹11,200 can boost equity toward the age-32 allocation (~68%).

Screenshot-style advisor card. Suggestions follow the app’s debt-vs-investing rule: expensive debt first, not “stop every SIP”.

This walkthrough is educational, not personal advice. OpenAI output can vary; the numbers above are the deterministic picture from Arjun’s sample records. Always check lender prepayment rules and a qualified adviser before you act on your own money.
