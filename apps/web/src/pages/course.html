Financial Freedom Journey

Thirty lessons that teach money, then send the user back into their own numbers.

This is the planned education module for Freedom Planner — not a video campus bolted on the side. Each lesson explains a concept, checks understanding, applies it to the household already in the app, and creates an action (a goal, a budget change, a debt plan). The in-app Learning hub today is still static reads. This page is the outline we will build toward.

See the 30 lessons Product walkthrough

The product loop

Learn → understand → look at your finances → use a Freedom Planner tool → take action → track improvement → next concept. Users are not forced through Lesson 1…30 in order. The app should recommend the next lesson from real gaps (high-interest debt before investing, empty emergency fund before FIRE).

  1. 1
    Learn

    5–8 minute concept.

  2. 2
    Check

    Short understanding check.

  3. 3
    Apply

    Use existing income, EMIs, SIPs.

  4. 4
    Act

    Create a goal or change a plan.

  5. 5
    Track

    Watch the gap close in the app.

Do not isolate this as “Courses”. Lessons reuse Financial Health, cash flow, loans, insurance, SIPs, goals, retirement, FIRE, Freedom Score, calculators, and the AI advisor. Do not ask the user to re-enter numbers the app already has.

Three levels

Foundation, Growth, and Freedom. Internally these map to beginner, intermediate, and advanced. About ten lessons each.

Foundation

Take control of your money. Understand the current situation and build a base: cash flow, budget, emergency fund, first debt plan. Lessons 1–10.

Growth

Build and protect wealth. Credit, interest burden, insurance, saving vs investing, inflation, SIP and asset classes. Lessons 11–20.

Freedom

Path to independence. Goals, retirement corpus, FIRE variants, freedom rate, and a personal roadmap. Lessons 21–30.

What one lesson looks like

Same skeleton every time. Example from the spec: “What is an emergency fund?”

  1. 1
    Learn

    Short lesson: why a buffer exists, emergency vs non-emergency, liquidity.

  2. 2
    Check

    e.g. how many months of essential expenses a fund generally covers.

  3. 3
    Apply

    Pull essential expenses from the household. Show target vs current vs gap (example in the spec: target ₹3,60,000, current ₹1,20,000, gap ₹2,40,000).

  4. 4
    Act

    Create the goal “Build ₹2,40,000 emergency fund” from that gap.

  5. 5
    Track

    Progress lives on Goals / Overview, then the next recommended lesson.
